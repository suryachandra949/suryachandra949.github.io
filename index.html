About

Suryachandra Prasad Pasupuleti

I am a PhD researcher in Computer Science at the University of York, working on energy-aware performance engineering of high-performance computing (HPC) workloads.

My research explores how to balance computational performance with energy efficiency through models and tools for energy-aware optimisation. My broader interests span systems for machine learning and machine learning for systems: making ML workloads more efficient, and using ML and large language models to support performance engineering, resource management, and other HPC tasks.

Before starting my PhD in September 2024, I worked as an FPGA Design Consultant in the UK, developing systems for avionics and high-speed communications. My experience spans FPGA-based acceleration, embedded systems, and hardware/software co-design, including deep learning inference and real-time image processing.

I hold an MSc in Digital Systems Engineering from the University of York and degrees in Electrical and Electronics Engineering and Chemistry from BITS Pilani. I have also held a research internship at IIIT Hyderabad.

BODHI: Intelligent and Explainable LLM-Assisted HPC Scheduling

Suryachandra Prasad Pasupuleti and Steven Wright

To appear in Proceedings of the 33rd IEEE International Conference on High Performance Computing (HiPC 2026).
Conference dates: 16–19 December 2026 · Bengaluru, India

BODHI combines the Flux scheduler with open-source language models to make context-aware HPC scheduling decisions and explain the trade-offs between resource efficiency and fairness. We evaluate 10 models and find that quantised mixture-of-experts models offer the best balance of scheduling quality, inference speed, and energy use. In our experiments, LLM-assisted scheduling improves user-level wait-time fairness over a utilisation-focused approach while retaining efficiency gains over FCFS and EASY backfilling.

Conference
Read abstract

Job scheduling on High-Performance Computing (HPC) systems involves optimising for different metrics such as utilisation, fairness, wait times, job throughput, and makespan, and is considered an NP-hard problem; often these metrics are conflicting in nature. As software workloads continue to evolve and hardware resources become increasingly complex, there is a growing need for intelligent schedulers capable of making context-aware scheduling decisions. Large language models (LLMs) offer a promising approach by reasoning over system state, multiple feasible scheduling solutions, job queue information, resource availability, and historical scheduling data to optimise multiple scheduling objectives simultaneously. In this paper, we introduce BODHI, an LLM-assisted scheduling framework built for the Flux scheduler to investigate the use of open-source LLMs for intelligent HPC job scheduling. We evaluate 10 open-source LLMs for the task of HPC job scheduling using both output quality and inference efficiency metrics, demonstrating that quantised Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models provide the best trade-off between scheduling quality, inference throughput, and energy consumption. While some models achieve strong performance in generating appropriate scheduling actions and coherent reasoning, all evaluated models show comparatively weaker performance in analysing and justifying trade-offs among the multiple feasible scheduling solutions. Finally, we compare two variants of our BODHI framework, LLM-assisted and utilisation-focused without LLM, against first-come-first-served (FCFS) and EASY backfilling. The utilisation-focused variant achieves the highest resource efficiency but at the cost of wait-time fairness. BODHI’s LLM-assisted scheduling recovers much of this fairness loss, particularly across users, while retaining efficiency gains well above FCFS and EASY.

The Performance-Power Frontier: A Model-Driven Approach to Energy-Aware Application Optimisation

Suryachandra Prasad Pasupuleti and Steven Wright

Proceedings of the 40th ACM International Conference on Supercomputing

We extend the Power-Optimised Software Envelope (POSE) model and introduce a visualisation toolkit to help performance engineers balance execution time and energy consumption. Experiments with the NAS Parallel Benchmarks show how opportunities for energy-aware optimisation vary across workloads, and when aggressive power or frequency limits can reduce efficiency.

PDF (accepted manuscript) Published paper Code / data
Read abstract

Energy consumption in high-performance computing (HPC) has emerged as a primary design constraint, with Tier-0 systems now demanding tens of megawatts. While users are increasingly being encouraged to minimise the environmental and operational costs of their workloads, they frequently lack the analytical frameworks required to navigate the complex trade-offs between execution time and energy efficiency. In this paper, we extend the Power-Optimised Software Envelope (POSE) model and introduce a visualisation tool designed to help performance engineers explore the energy-time design space. By aligning multi-objective optimisation metrics with institutional operational priorities, our framework enables policy-aware decision-making at the application level. We demonstrate this approach by analysing the NAS Parallel Benchmarks. Our results reveal a significant variance in energy-aware optimisation potential: while the Embarrassingly Parallel (EP) kernel shows negligible opportunity for gains (up to 0.37% improvement in Energy-Delay Summation), the Conjugate Gradient (CG) kernel offers greater scope (up to a 12.96% improvement). Furthermore, we study the benchmarks under frequency and power constraints and identify critical efficiency thresholds; for the Block Tri-Diagonal (BT) solver, configurations operating below 2.00 GHz or under a 140 W powercap fail to outperform the baseline efficiency, highlighting the risks of aggressive over-throttling.
